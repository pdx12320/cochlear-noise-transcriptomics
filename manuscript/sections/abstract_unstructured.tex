\section*{Abstract}
Acoustic trauma is associated with divergent inflammatory transcription across cochlear stromal, vascular and immune populations. We analyzed replicated single-cell profiles alongside whole-cochlea transcriptomes and complementary expression-localization data from public mouse datasets. Fibrocyte/\allowbreak{}mesenchymal populations showed tumor necrosis factor--nuclear factor \(\kappa\)B (TNF--NF-\(\kappa\)B) enrichment toward noise exposure in the lateral wall and spiral ganglion region. Endothelial and immune/\allowbreak{}myeloid populations showed enrichment toward the unexposed condition. These enrichment directions persisted after omission of individual animals from the models. Chemokine-associated transcription was prominent in the lateral-wall fibrocyte response. Lateral-wall endothelial cells showed mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) family enrichment with a prominent proteasome component. Whole-cochlea profiles showed inflammatory, protein-folding and unfolded-protein-response enrichment after exposure. A subset of candidate genes retained recurrent endothelial or fibrocyte/\allowbreak{}mesenchymal mean-expression maxima across pooled condition libraries, including under the alternative cluster annotation. Stromal inflammatory and chemokine transcription coexists with vascular protein-quality-control programs in the cochlear response to noise.

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} acoustic trauma; cochlea; single-cell RNA sequencing; pseudobulk; fibrocytes; NF-\(\kappa\)B
